Tukar

Send money home privately. Cash out in local fiat.

USDC in · private crossing · local fiat out

A mobile-first cross-border remittance app on Stellar. A worker deposits USDC, the crossing hides the amount and both people, the family cashes out in local fiat. What sets it apart from every other privacy app: it stays provable to a regulator. Private in the middle, accountable at the edges.

Problem → solution

A public ledger leaks who moved the money.
Tukar keeps the crossing private.

Cross-border remittance is Stellar's core use case, but on a public ledger every payment's amount and both counterparties are exposed. A corridor has to hide the parties too, while staying auditable enough for a regulator.

01 · PUBLIC
Deposit
Real testnet USDC enters the pool. A ZK compliance proof shows the authenticated depositor is allow-listed.
02 · PRIVATE
Shielded crossing
The transfer hides the amount and both counterparties on-chain. Only a commitment and nullifiers are visible.
03 · PUBLIC
Off-ramp
The receiver reveals only at the edge and withdraws to local fiat (IDR, PHP, VND, THB, INR, MXN, BRL, ARS, NGN, COP).
04 · ON DEMAND
Disclose
A holder proves one fact to a regulator (e.g. the amount), with no keys and no other payments.

Who it's for: the migrant worker sending money home and the family receiving it in local currency, plus the licensed anchors and PSPs that pay for a private corridor they can still audit. Public at the edges by design, private exactly where a corridor must not leak.

The moat

Compliant privacy on cross-border money. No catalog equivalent.

The category already has privacy tools and consumer wallets. None sits where Tukar does: private for the user and provable to a regulator, on a real remittance corridor.

vs a privacy pool / mixer

Compliance is in the circuit

A mixer is private but not accountable. Every Tukar deposit proves in-circuit that the authenticated depositor is ASP allow-listed and not deny-listed, key pinned to from. Compliance is in the proof, not a promise.

vs a private payment link

A real cross-border corridor

Payment links move USDC between crypto users. Tukar takes real fiat in and out through live SEP anchors on Stellar's rail, 10 corridors, with an on-chain Reflector min-receive gate on the off-ramp rate.

vs a plain consumer wallet

Private, and it verifies

Wallets are cross-border but public. Tukar hides the amount and both parties on-chain, and a regulator can verify a disclosed fact live; tamper with it and the Stellar verifier returns InvalidProof.

The combination

The layer anchors plug into

Each rival covers one axis. Holding all three at once has no off-the-shelf equivalent, so Tukar isn't another privacy app, it's the compliant settlement layer a licensed anchor plugs into, one policy per corridor.

The opportunity

A $669B market that still pays over 6% to move.

Cross-border money is huge, slow, and expensive. Crypto rails made it cheap but public, and that trade is exactly what regulated money can't take. Tukar closes the gap.

The market

$669B a year

Remittances into low- and middle-income countries in 2023 (World Bank). Sending $200 still averages about 6.2%, more than double the UN's 3% target, and it has barely moved in a decade.

Why it's still open

Cheap or compliant, not both

Public stablecoin rails cut the fee but expose every amount and counterparty. Privacy tools hide it but can't answer a regulator. Neither is usable for a licensed corridor.

Business model

A rail, not another app to acquire

Tukar is the private settlement leg between anchors. Revenue is a thin take-rate on settlement volume, paid by the anchors and PSPs that route through it. B2B2C, so we don't buy consumers one at a time.

Go to market

One corridor, one anchor, first

Launch a single high-volume lane with one licensed anchor, then add corridors. The post-hackathon path is a Stellar Community Fund build award on the same rail.

Reflector · SEP-40 FX oracle

Oracle-gated settlement

The off-ramp rate is read on-chain by the pool, priced on the median of 5 records so one manipulated spot can't move it. The withdraw carries a min-receive gate: the pool re-reads Reflector at settlement and refuses to release below ~99% of the quote, failing closed if the feed stalls.

Real assets · real setup

Real USDC, real ceremony

The pool custodies real testnet USDC: deposit moves the amount you type, withdraw releases it, and the commitment binds to exactly that amount. All seven proving keys come from a real multi-party trusted setup (Hermez Powers-of-Tau), independently verifiable with snarkjs zkey verify.

Verified on-chain, not asserted

Genuine passes, tampered fails

A genuine disclosure verifies against the live Stellar verifier. Tamper with the disclosed amount and it returns InvalidProof. A cross-wallet double-spend is rejected on-chain as NullifierUsed.
