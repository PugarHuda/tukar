The problem

Stellar moves real money.
A public ledger leaks who moved it.

Cross-border remittance is Stellar's core real-world use case. But on a public ledger, every payment's amount and both counterparties are exposed, leaking a sensitive financial graph: who pays whom, how much, how often.

Hiding balances alone doesn't fix a remittance corridor. If the corridor still reveals sender → receiver, the payment graph is public. A corridor must hide the parties too, while staying auditable enough for a regulator.

The solution

Private in the middle, accountable at the edges.

01 · PUBLIC
Deposit
Real testnet USDC enters the pool. A ZK compliance proof shows the authenticated depositor is allow-listed.
02 · PRIVATE
Shielded crossing
The transfer hides the amount and both counterparties on-chain. Only a commitment and nullifiers are visible.
03 · PUBLIC
Off-ramp
The receiver reveals only at the edge and withdraws to local fiat (IDR, PHP, VND, THB, INR, MXN, BRL, ARS, NGN, COP).
04 · ON DEMAND
Disclose
A holder proves one fact to a regulator (e.g. the amount), with no keys and no other payments.

Deposits and withdrawals are public by design (Privacy-Pools model). The private leg is the crossing, exactly where a corridor must not leak.

Ecosystem integration, real not decorative

The oracle moves funds. The anchors are live.

Reflector · SEP-40 FX oracle

Load-bearing, not display

For MX/BR/AR + THB (Thailand) the off-ramp rate is read on-chain by the pool. The withdraw carries a min-receive gate: the pool re-reads Reflector at settlement and refuses to release below ~99% of the quote, failing closed if the feed stalls. It prices on the median of 5 records, so one manipulated spot can't move it.

What's real (not mocked)

Real USDC. Real custody. Real setup.

  • Real testnet USDC custody. deposit moves the actual amount you type; withdraw releases it.
  • Amount ↔ commitment binding. A deposit proves the commitment opens to exactly the deposited amount. Privacy can't decouple hidden value from moved tokens.
  • Recipient bound into the proof. The contract recomputes keccak256(recipient ‖ amount), so a withdraw proof can't be replayed to a different recipient.
  • Fully trustless tree. The root advances only via a merkleUpdate proof. No admin override. Leaves live in durable state, so any client rebuilds the exact tree from chain.
  • Real trusted setup. All four proving keys derive from the Hermez Powers-of-Tau ceremony, independently verifiable: snarkjs zkey verify → ZKey Ok! ×4.
  • Bearer notes + payment requests. A note is spendable value: export it as a string/QR, import on another device, withdraw. Requests pull money the other way.

Honest scope

What this is and isn't.

Hackathon-grade and security-hardened on testnet through many adversarial self-audit rounds (19-point threat model). Not professionally audited. Do not use with real assets.

  • Fiat anchors. The SEP protocols are really integrated against SDF's reference anchor, but a production ramp needs a licensed KYC anchor. A business problem, not a code problem.
  • Trusted setup phase-2. The live keys are the output of a multi-party ceremony (3 contributions + beacon), byte-identical to the committed transcripts. We ran the rounds on one machine to prove the process; full one-honest-party soundness needs genuinely independent contributors.
  • Anonymity set. Privacy scales with pool usage. A demo-scale pool has a small set; that's inherent to the model, and we say so.
  • The shared demo key is public. This means the no-install demo isn't access-controlled. The design is correct for real wallets (connect Freighter and it is).
